## Executive Summary

Independent florists grow profit fastest by improving **four metrics** before chasing more traffic:

1. **Average order value (AOV)** — upsells, premium anchors, add-ons
2. **Repeat customer rate** — subscriptions, occasion reminders, corporate accounts
3. **Gross margin %** — pricing discipline, recipe costing, waste control
4. **Revenue mix** — shift from low-margin wire/aggregator orders to direct sales

Dandelion already has strong assets: local brand since 2010, East Bay delivery, subscriptions, weddings, and memorials. The strategic opportunity is to **convert one-time web buyers into higher-value repeat customers** while **raising AOV and protecting margin** on every order.

Website/CRO work (Week 1 guide) supports conversion — this document focuses on **where revenue and margin actually come from** and **which levers to pull first**.

## Industry Context (2025–2026)

### What top-performing independent florists do differently

| Practice | Why it matters |
|----------|----------------|
| **Direct relationships over wire services** | FTD / 1-800-Flowers / Teleflora can take 30–50% of delivery revenue in fees. Same $75 order direct ≈ $55–65 vs. $37–52 through wire. |
| **Raise prices when costs rise** | Shops that held prices during peak holidays absorbed margin loss; those that recosted and adjusted pricing performed better. |
| **2–3 priorities, not 8** | Focus beats scattered marketing. Pick one financial, one customer, and one channel priority per quarter. |
| **Subscriptions + occasion reminders** | Recurring customers often generate $350–1,100/year vs. $180–350 for one-time wire orders. 25–40% of one-time buyers can convert to direct repeat within 12 months with structured follow-up. |
| **Price anchoring** | Show premium designs ($300–400) next to mid-tier ($85–110) to lift mid-tier sales without discounting. |
| **Corporate / house accounts** | Banks, law firms, hotels, hospitals — steady volume between holidays, higher AOV, predictable billing. |
| **Recipe-level costing** | Margin leaks when stem counts, labor, and vessel depreciation aren’t in COGS. Recost at current wholesale + freight, not last year’s numbers. |

### Dandelion’s current product signals (from site audit)

| Signal | Strategic implication |
|--------|----------------------|

## The Four Revenue Levers (Priority Framework)

Use this to diagnose *which* problem you have before choosing tactics.

```
                    ┌─────────────────────────────────┐
                    │         TOTAL REVENUE           │
                    └─────────────────────────────────┘
                           │         │         │
              ┌────────────┘         │         └────────────┐
              ▼                      ▼                      ▼
        ┌───────────┐         ┌───────────┐         ┌───────────┐
        │  TRAFFIC  │         │    AOV    │         │  REPEAT   │
        │  (orders) │    ×    │  ($/order)│    ×    │  (freq)   │
        └───────────┘         └───────────┘         └───────────┘
              │                      │                      │
              └──────────────────────┼──────────────────────┘
                                     ▼
                           ┌─────────────────┐
                           │  GROSS MARGIN % │
                           │  (profit/order) │
                           └─────────────────┘
```

### Diagnostic questions

| If this is weak… | You likely have… | Fix first… |
|------------------|------------------|------------|
| Traffic / order count | Awareness or conversion problem | Local SEO, Google Business, paid local, website CRO |
| AOV | Pricing / offer structure problem | Premium anchors, bundles, add-ons, minimums |
| Repeat rate | Retention problem | Subscriptions, email/SMS reminders, post-delivery follow-up |
| Margin % | Costing / operations problem | Recost recipes, reduce waste, delivery route efficiency, price increases |

**Rule:** If revenue is down but AOV is healthy → traffic/conversion. If revenue is flat but repeat is rising → you're building a base. If revenue is up but margin is down → stop and fix pricing/costing before scaling volume.

## Revenue Streams — How to Analyze Each

When you share data, we'll segment by channel. Below is what to measure and typical benchmarks for independent shops.

### 1. Everyday retail (walk-in + web delivery)

| Metric | What to track | Benchmark / target |
|--------|---------------|-------------------|
| Orders per week (non-holiday) | Baseline demand | Establish your Tue–Fri “normal” |
| AOV | Total retail ÷ order count | Independent shops often $65–95; goal +15–20% via add-ons |
| % online vs. phone vs. walk-in | Channel mix | Track shift toward self-serve web |
| Delivery cost per order | Driver time + fuel ÷ deliveries | Should be <12–15% of order value |
| Add-on attach rate | % orders with card, vase upgrade, etc. | Target 25–40% for card alone |

**Dandelion opportunities:** Checkout card upsell ($6.50), vase upgrades, “deluxe” vs. “standard” variants on top SKUs, same-day premium fee.

### 2. Subscriptions

| Metric | What to track | Why |
|--------|---------------|-----|
| Active subscribers | Count by tier (weekly / monthly / 12-mo) | Core recurring revenue |
| MRR / ARR | Monthly × 12 | Predictable cash flow |
| Churn rate | Cancellations ÷ active base | >5%/mo needs attention |
| CAC for sub signups | Marketing ÷ new subs | Should pay back in 2–3 deliveries |
| Margin per delivery | COGS + labor + delivery per sub run | Subs fail when delivery density is low |

**Dandelion opportunities:** Fix “12 Months” variant naming, promote “You’re My Every Week!!” and monthly tiers on homepage, referral incentive for existing subs.

### 3. Weddings & events

| Metric | What to track | Why |
|--------|---------------|-----|
| Inquiries per month | Form + phone + walk-in | Top-of-funnel |
| Close rate | Booked ÷ inquiries | Industry often 20–35% for qualified leads |
| Average wedding revenue | Total wedding sales ÷ events | Often $3k–15k+ depending on market |
| Labor hours per event | Design + install ÷ revenue | Margin killer if not priced in |
| Deposit / payment terms | % collected upfront | Cash flow protection |

**Dandelion opportunities:** Replace phone-only inquiry with form + portfolio, tiered packages (elopement / classic / full), minimum spend for peak dates.

### 4. Memorial / sympathy

| Metric | What to track | Why |
|--------|---------------|-----|
| Orders per month | Steady vs. seasonal | Often under-marketed but high-intent |
| AOV vs. everyday | Sympathy skews higher | Standing sprays, casket pieces |
| Funeral home relationships | Orders per partner | B2B referral channel |
| On-time delivery rate | Critical for trust | One miss damages referral pipeline |

**Dandelion opportunities:** Required checkout fields (service date/time/location), funeral home outreach, sympathy-specific landing page SEO.

### 5. Corporate / house accounts

| Metric | What to track | Why |
|--------|---------------|-----|
| Active accounts | # businesses on net-30 terms | Steady between holidays |
| Revenue per account / month | Recurring lobby, events, gifts | Often highest margin when routinized |
| Days to pay | AR aging | Working capital |

**Dandelion opportunities:** Outbound to Alameda/Oakland offices, hospitals, hotels; branded weekly lobby program.

### 6. Wire / third-party orders (if any)

| Metric | What to track | Why |
|--------|---------------|-----|
| % of revenue from wire | FTD, Teleflora, BloomNation, etc. | Fee drag |
| Net margin per wire order | After all fees | Often negative or break-even |
| Conversion to direct | % wire senders who reorder direct | Retention play |

**Strategic default:** Minimize wire dependence; use incoming wire only for geographic reach, never as primary revenue.

## Margin Protection — Non-Negotiables

Revenue growth without margin discipline is a trap. Before scaling marketing:

1. **Recost top 10 SKUs** at current wholesale + freight + labor + vessel
2. **Set minimum markup** by category (everyday / sympathy / wedding / sub)
3. **Track shrink and waste** weekly (unsold stems, failed deliveries, remakes)
4. **Price for delivery** — low-AOV distant ZIPs may need minimum order or delivery fee
5. **Seasonal price adjustments** — Valentine’s, Mother’s Day, wedding season

Target gross margin for healthy independent retail florists: **45–60%** blended (weddings/events may differ).
